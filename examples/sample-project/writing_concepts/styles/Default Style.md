# Default Style

Default style of the Writing Room.

---

## 1. Narrative Voice

- **Point of View:** Third person omniscient
- **Tense:** Present
- **Narrator Distance:** Moderate — balanced interiority and external observation
- **Narrator Reliability:** Reliable
- **Narrator Presence:** Transparent — narrator is invisible, no personality
- **Free Indirect Discourse:** No — interiority is always clearly marked (italics, "she thought", etc.)

---

## 2. Vocabulary

- **Register:** Conversational
- **Complexity:** Moderate — varied but not ostentatious
- **Domain / Period Language:** None — contemporary neutral
- **Consistency Across POVs:** All POVs share the same vocabulary register

## 3. Sentence Structure

- **Typical Length:** Medium — 12–20 words average
- **Dominant Syntax:** Subject-verb-object (direct, declarative)
- **Subordinate Clauses:** Moderate — used for nuance and pacing
- **Parallelism and Lists:** Use sparingly for emphasis
- **Sentence Fragments:** Permitted in action sequences only
- **Rhetorical Patterns:** <List any recurring syntactic devices that define this style. Examples: anaphora ("She had not eaten. She had not slept. She had not stopped."), chiasmus, deliberate repetition for rhythm.>

---

## 4. Paragraph Structure

- **Typical Length:** Medium — 5–8 sentences
- **Single-Sentence Paragraphs:** Permitted for emphasis only — use sparingly
- **Scene vs. Summary Balance:** Balanced — mix of scene and narrative summary
- **Paragraph Openings:** Avoid starting consecutive paragraphs with the same word or construction
- **Transitions Between Paragraphs:** Soft transitions — light connective tissue between paragraphs
- **White Space / Section Breaks:** No section breaks within chapters

---

## 5. Chapter Structure

- **Typical Length:** 1,500–3,000 words
- **POV per Chapter:** Single POV — one character per chapter, shifts permitted at section breaks
- **Chapter Titles:** None — numbered only
- **Chapter Opening (default):** Establishing beat — brief grounding in place and character before action begins
- **Chapter Ending (default):** Resolution — chapter completes its own mini-arc before the story continues
- **Pacing Within a Chapter (default):** Even — consistent pacing throughout

> **Note for the Planner:** `Chapter Opening`, `Chapter Ending`, and `Pacing Within a Chapter` are style defaults. Individual chapter plans may override any of these three fields for that chapter. The Draft Writer always defers to the chapter plan if an override is present; otherwise falls back to the defaults above.

---

## 6. Dialogue

- **Dialogue Tags:** "said" and "asked" only — no other attribution verbs
- **Adverbs on Tags:** Sparingly — only when manner cannot be shown otherwise
- **Dialect and Accent:** Light phonetic marking permitted — dropped g's, contractions
- **Subtext:** Mixed — subtext is character-dependent
- **Conversation Rhythm:** Mixed — alternates short exchanges with longer turns; speeches permitted when the scene requires it, but must not become exposition dumps
- **Internal Monologue:** Italics with attribution — *She was lying*, she thought
- **Punctuation Conventions:** Standard — comma before tag, period for action beat

---

## 7. Description

- **Descriptive Density:** Moderate — key details selected for atmosphere and grounding
- **Sensory Priority:** Visual-dominant
- **Metaphor and Simile:** Moderate — woven throughout
- **Metaphor Source:** Grounded in the POV character's world and experience — a farmer's metaphors come from farming, a soldier's from war
- **Character Description:** Gradual — physical details emerge over several scenes
- **Setting Description:** Establishing — dedicated description block when entering a new location
- **Describing Other Characters Through POV:** Objective — POV character describes others neutrally

---

## 8. Tone / Mood

- **Overall Register:** Neutral (primary), Hopeful (secondary)
- **Consistency:** Character-dependent — each POV has its own tonal register
- **Emotional Distance:** Neutral — narrator observes without editorialising
- **Humour:** Dry wit — understated, situational

- **Tension Release:** Quiet character moments

## 9. Forbidden Elements

This section is a hard checklist. The Draft Writer and Draft Fixer treat every item here as a prohibition, not a preference — fixes must comply with the style as strictly as original prose. The Reviewer-Quality agent uses this list when evaluating drafts.

### Prose Patterns to Avoid
- Throat-clearing openings — do not begin a chapter or scene with weather, waking up, or looking in a mirror
- Passive constructions used to avoid agency — "mistakes were made", "the door was opened"
- Over-explained emotion — do not state what a character feels immediately after showing it ("She clenched her fists. She was furious.")
- Simultaneous actions that are physically impossible — "Nodding, she stood up and crossed the room while turning to face him"

### Words and Phrases to Avoid
- Adverbs on dialogue tags: "whispered softly", "said quietly", "muttered darkly"
- Filler intensifiers: "very", "really", "quite", "rather", "just", "so"
- AI-slop abstractions: "a mix of emotions", "a wave of relief", "a pang of guilt", "something shifted inside her"
- Melodramatic standbys: "little did she know", "that was when everything changed", "nothing would ever be the same"

### Structural Patterns to Avoid
- Dream sequences used to deliver exposition or foreshadowing
- Flashbacks longer than one paragraph without a clear narrative reason
- Characters explaining things they both already know purely for the reader's benefit
- Chain of "and" phrases. For example: "He was tall, and handsome, and slim, and smart". Only use for strong emphasis (e.g, "The party was boring, and crowded, and noisy" to express discomfort). Otherwise use simple commas (e.g, "He was tall, handsome, slim and smart").

### Tonal Registers to Avoid
- Sentimentality — earned emotion only; do not reach for feeling the prose has not justified
- Ironic detachment applied to moments that require sincerity
